Questions

Custom Cornice & Crown Molding — FAQ

What is the difference between cornice and crown molding?

Crown molding is a specific shaped profile where the wall meets the ceiling. Cornice is the broader term for the finished crown assembly — it can be a single molding or a built-up stack of several pieces, and it also refers to the decorative molding at the roofline on a home's exterior. We fabricate and install both, interior and exterior.

How much does custom cornice cost in Houston?

Trim work is priced per linear foot and depends on the profile complexity, material (MDF vs. hardwood), ceiling height, and how much prep the walls need. Send us your plans or invite us for an on-site measure — you'll get a written, line-item quote, typically within one business day.

Should I choose MDF or hardwood for crown molding?

MDF is the standard for paint-grade trim: it's stable, perfectly smooth, and less expensive — ideal for most interior crown and cornice. Choose stain-grade hardwood when you want a stained or natural finish, or for areas that take abuse. For exterior cornice we use rot-resistant materials like PVC or cedar.

Can you match existing trim in an older home?

Yes — matching original molding is one of our most-requested jobs in The Heights, Montrose, and West University. We copy the existing profile with a knives-cut match or a built-up assembly so new rooms, additions, and repaired sections are indistinguishable from the original woodwork.

How long does trim installation take?

A single room of crown typically takes a day or two including caulking and fit-finish. Whole-house packages run a week or more depending on linear footage, ceiling heights, and profile complexity. You get a schedule with your written estimate.
